# Appendix B range equations used in the replay

Write $r=|x|$, $n=x/r$, $P=I-nn^t$, $\lambda=l(l+1)$. The reference is [Czimek, arXiv:1609.08814v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.08814v2), §§4.3 and 5.3, with the sign of the scalar source reversed as prescribed in Paper 3, §B.3. In the following derivation radial components are in physical frames; the angular basis uses a unit-sphere normalized real harmonic Y. For tangential vectors we use positive $D Y/s,n\times D Y/s$, $s=\sqrt\lambda$. Thus converting the electric harmonic from the reference changes the sign of its coefficient. The sign is also fixed directly by $\mathrm{div}_{S^2}DY=-\lambda Y$.

## Two explicit counterexamples to identifying the old range

At the flat metric the polar map has first variation $2\phi P+n\otimes\beta+\beta\otimes n$, so its normal-normal component vanishes. For a nonnegative nonzero radial curvature source f supported in (2,3) the old metric inverse gives $A(r)= (2r)^{-1}\int_1^r s^2 f(s)ds$ in that component. Thus the two ranges differ even in the radial sector and after radial weighting.

For pure radial-vector forcing $f(r)Y_{20}n$ the old momentum inverse has $e=0$, $a=r^{-3}\int_1^r s^3 f(s)ds$, $dT_E=Da/2$. Lemma B.10 requires $dT_E=-Da/2$ outside the collar. Since $Da\ne0$ on an open patch for r>3, those ranges also differ. This counterexample identifies the implementations and does not contradict the paper.

## Weighted selection without residual-space Neumann iteration

Use $w=\exp(-0.001\ell)$, with $\ell=\chi_0(2r-3)\log r$ and a smooth unit transition. If a correction U satisfies its forced equation, zero inner jets and the selected range relations for $w^{-1}U$, then the critical-weight inverse applied to $P(w^{-1}U)$ recovers $w^{-1}U$. Indeed the following sector equations select the same profiles, determined by the zero inner data and the exclusion of the growing homogeneous mode. This characterizes $w\operatorname{Ran}B$ directly on the tested sectors. The operator norm threshold for an all-data Neumann inverse is not a numerical input. The finite system solves for its actual coefficient without using a universal norm bound.

Write $t=\log r$, dot for $d/dt$. The scalar unknowns are $(\phi,j)=(\phi,\dot\phi+\phi)$, with homogeneous matrix $[[-1,1],[\lambda/2,-1]]$. Degree zero uses zero inner data alone. For positive degree put $v=\sqrt{\lambda/2}$, $q(r)=\chi(l(r-1))$, and define
\[
d=-\frac{\int r^{v+1}q'}{(v+1)\int r^v q'},\quad
z=r^{v-2}(q'-dq''),\quad G(r)=\int_1^r s^3z(s)\,ds .
\]
The integration variable in both integrals defining d is r. The weighted physical shift coefficient is $-CwG/(s r^2)$. It has zero curl and vanishes off the collar. The scalar forcing in the second row is $r^2[-H/4+Cw(z+(w'/w)G/r^3)/2]$. At the outer edge the condition $v\phi+j=0$ removes the growing mode of the zero-source continuation. The first profile moment is fixed by decay; the second moment, $G(\infty)=0$, fixes d. In particular the unweighted first moment gives $C=\tfrac12\int r^{2-v}H\,dr$. The two integral powers include the $r^{-1}$ implicit in the reference harmonics.

For the electric momentum let $(a,e)$ be the normal and normalized gradient coefficients. Above degree one the matrix in t is $[[-3,s],[s,-2]]$ and the forcing $r(f_N,f_E+Cw r^{l-2}q')$. The row $(l/s,1)$ kills the growing coefficient at the outer boundary. The weighted range then has $dT_E=-Da/2-e-r\zeta_E^w$ in vector notation. In degree one remove the tensor component altogether: the matrix is $[[-3,s],[s/2,-3]]$, both exponents decay. For degree zero $\dot a+3a=rf_N$.

For the rotated-gradient mode let b be its mixed coefficient and $j=b+(dT)_H+r\zeta_H^w$. Put $\nu=\sqrt{\lambda+4}$, $\zeta_H^w=Cw r^\nu q'$. The coupled system has matrix $[[-2,-1],[-\lambda,2+\dot w/w]]$ and forcing $(r(f_H+\zeta_H^w),0)$. It follows by applying the coexact range equation of Lemma B.10 to $w^{-1}U$, then substituting the physical divergence. At the outer radius $\dot w/w=-0.001$ and the coefficient along the growing eigenvector is zero. For degree one b solves $\dot b+3b=r f_H$. Tensor reconstruction uses $d(\operatorname{Hess}^{TF}Y)=(2-\lambda)DY/2$ and its symmetric rotation. For w=1 the zero-growing-mode equation reproduces precisely the prescribed signed source moment in each sector.

Nonlinear terms use the polar metric of §B.1 with unchanged lapse and $K=\mathrm{TF}_{h}(\hat a+v)$. The computation iterates the two coupled residual equations to a finite truncation. It solves the boundary-value equations for the selected parameters directly. The differential residual readout uses the full Cartesian Ricci and covariant momentum expressions, independently of the modal ODE used in the inverse. Exterior sources beyond the computational aperture are set to zero in each update, with an analytic homogeneous boundary condition for the linear inverse. The output is tested as a finite approximation. The comparison of two apertures measures sensitivity to the truncation.

## Homogeneous continuation at the outer edge

Each finite inverse step extends its source by zero for $r>R$. The boundary row is imposed on the exact homogeneous radial continuation, rather than setting its solution to zero at $R$. In the scalar sector the surviving exponent is $-1-\sqrt{\lambda/2}$ for $l\ge1$; the zero sector has $(A+B\log r)/r$. The shift is zero outside its compact collar. The electric momentum degree zero decays as $r^{-3}$, degree one has powers $r^{-2},r^{-4}$, and the other degrees retain the exponent $-l-3$. For magnetic degree one the exponent is $-3$; for higher degrees it is $-\epsilon_\gamma/2-\sqrt{\lambda+(2-\epsilon_\gamma/2)^2}$. These follow from the eigenvalues of the displayed matrices. In particular these finite angular tails have the required weighted decay at infinity for any fixed derivative order. This describes each exterior linear continuation. The nonlinear residual beyond the aperture is an additional truncation error. Its quadratic products at large $r$ are $O(r^{-4}(1+\log r)^2)$ in the Hamiltonian and $O(r^{-4}(1+\log r))$ in momentum. For example the second-derivative remainder is bounded by a constant times $|h-I|\,|\partial^2h|+|\partial h|^2$, and the momentum remainder by $|h-I||\partial K|+|\partial h||K|$. These are asymptotic orders, with constants depending on the output. The computations at $R=8,16$ test sensitivity to the discarded nonlinear source.
